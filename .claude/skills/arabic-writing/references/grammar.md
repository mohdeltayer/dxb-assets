## 1. Sentence types and word order

Arabic has two sentence types, named for their first word.

- **Nominal sentence (الجملة الاسمية)** = subject (مبتدأ) + predicate (خبر), both **nominative**,
  with **no copula** in the present tense:
  - العِلْمُ نورٌ — "Knowledge is light."
  - السماءُ صافيةٌ — "The sky is clear."
- **Verbal sentence (الجملة الفعلية)** = default order **Verb–Subject–Object (VSO)** — the
  reverse of English:
  - كتبَ الطالبُ الدرسَ — "The student wrote the lesson."

Because case endings preserve the grammatical roles, order can shift for emphasis: VSO is the
neutral narrative/news default; SVO (الطالبُ كتبَ) topicalizes the subject; OVS fronts the
object. **Translationese tell:** mechanically using nominal/SVO order to mirror English where
a verbal VSO sentence is natural — ✗ الحكومةُ أعلنت → ✓ أعلنت الحكومةُ.

## 2. Case endings (الإعراب)

Three cases, marked by a final short vowel (usually unwritten but grammatically real):

| Case | Arabic | Definite marker | Indefinite (tanwīn) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | مرفوع | ـُ | ـٌ (-un) |
| Accusative | منصوب | ـَ | ـً (-an, + supporting alif) |
| Genitive | مجرور | ـِ | ـٍ (-in) |

- **Nominative:** the فاعل (subject of a verb), and both مبتدأ and خبر of a nominal sentence.
- **Accusative:** مفعول به (object), adverbials, tamyīz, ḥāl, the predicate of كان, the noun
  after إنّ.
- **Genitive:** after a preposition, and as the second term of an iḍāfa.

**High-frequency ending errors:**
- Dual nominative ـانِ → accusative/genitive ـينِ: الطالبانِ → رأيت الطالبَيْنِ.
- Sound masculine plural nominative ـونَ → ـينَ: المعلمونَ → رأيت المعلمينَ.
- Sound feminine plural accusative takes **kasra, not fatha**: رأيت المعلِّماتِ (never ـاتً).
- Diptotes (الممنوع من الصرف, e.g. أحمد، مساجد، صحراء) take **no tanwīn** and use **fatha** for
  the genitive: مررت بأحمدَ.

## 3. Gender and number agreement

**THE single most important rule:** any plural that does **not** denote rational humans
(جمع غير العاقل) — broken or sound — takes **feminine-singular** agreement on verbs,
adjectives, demonstratives, and pronouns:

- الكتبُ جديدةٌ — "The books are new." (not جُدُد)
- السياراتُ سريعةٌ — "The cars are fast."
- قرأتُ مقالاتٍ كثيرةً — "I read many articles."
- البيوتُ كبيرةٌ وهي جميلةٌ — "The houses are big and **they (هي, fem. sg.)** are beautiful."

Rational/human plurals take true plural agreement, matched for gender:
المعلِّمونَ مجتهدونَ / المعلِّماتُ مجتهداتٌ.

Other agreement axes: singular / **dual (المثنى)** / plural. The dual is obligatory for "two"
— كتابانِ ("two books"), not a plural; you cannot say "2 books" with a plural.

## 4. Verbs, tense/aspect, and the verb–subject order rule

Three base forms:
- **Past (الماضي):** كتبَ.
- **Present/imperfect (المضارع):** يكتبُ, with moods — indicative يكتبُ, subjunctive أن يكتبَ,
  jussive لم يكتبْ.
- **Imperative (الأمر):** اكتبْ.

Arabic is **aspectual** (completed vs ongoing), not richly tensed. Map English tenses by sense,
not form (see translationese.md): present perfect → قد + past (قد كتب)، past perfect → كان قد +
past، progressive → simple المضارع (optionally ما زال / لا يزال for "still").

**The order asymmetry (a top error source):**
- **Verb before subject (VSO):** the verb stays **singular** and agrees only in **gender**:
  - ذهبَ الطلابُ — "The students went."  ·  ذهبتِ الطالباتُ — "The female students went."
- **Subject before verb (SVO):** the verb shows **full** number + gender agreement:
  - الطلابُ ذهبوا  ·  المعلِّماتُ ذهبْنَ.
- ✗ ذهبوا الطلابُ (plural verb *before* plural subject) is the classic mistake.

## 5. Iḍāfa / construct state (الإضافة)

مضاف (the possessed head) + مضاف إليه (the possessor, always **genitive**):

- The **مضاف takes no ال and no tanwīn**: كتابُ الطالبِ — "the student's book"
  (✗ الكتابُ الطالبِ, ✗ كتابٌ الطالبِ).
- Definiteness flows from the final term: بابُ البيتِ — "the door of the house."
- **No adjective is inserted between the two parts** — it follows the whole construct and
  agrees with whichever noun it modifies:
  - سيارةُ المديرِ الجديدةُ — "the manager's **new car**."
  - سيارةُ المديرِ الجديدِ — "the **new manager's** car."
- In a chained iḍāfa only the last noun takes ال: بابُ بيتِ المديرِ.
- A sound plural / dual loses its final ن as a مضاف: معلِّمو المدرسةِ (not معلِّمونَ المدرسةِ),
  مديرا الشركةِ.

Use iḍāfa or a pronoun suffix for English possessives — **not** الخاص بـ:
"the company's policy" → سياسةُ الشركةِ, "your account" → حسابُك.

## 6. Definiteness and adjective agreement

The adjective **follows** its noun and agrees on **four axes**: gender, number, case, and
definiteness.

- البنتُ الكبيرةُ — "the big girl" (both definite) vs البنتُ كبيرةٌ — "the girl is big" (a
  nominal sentence). **Mismatched definiteness flips a phrase into a sentence** — a key
  correctness point.
- رأيتُ الطالبَ الجديدَ (both accusative); مررتُ بطلابٍ مجتهدينَ (both genitive indefinite).
- For non-human plurals the adjective is **feminine singular**: السياراتُ الجديدةُ.

## 7. Numbers and the counted noun (العدد والمعدود)

The hardest agreement system, governed by **gender polarity** (3–10 take the *opposite*
gender of the counted noun) plus shifting case/number of the counted noun (المعدود):

| Number | Number's gender | Counted noun: number | Counted noun: case |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | agrees with the noun | (the noun alone / dual) | follows the noun |
| 3–10 | **reverse polarity** | plural | genitive (iḍāfa) |
| 11–99 | mixed | singular | accusative (tamyīz) |
| 100, 1000 | invariable | singular | genitive (iḍāfa) |

- ثلاثةُ رجالٍ — "three men" (masc. noun → the number takes ة).
- ثلاثُ فتياتٍ — "three girls" (fem. noun → the number drops ة).
- أحدَ عشرَ رجلًا — "11 men"؛ أربعةَ عشرَ رجلًا — "14 men"؛ خمسٌ وعشرونَ امرأةً — "25 women"
  (counted noun singular accusative).
- مئةُ رجلٍ — "100 men"؛ ألفُ امرأةٍ — "1000 women" (singular genitive).

Note: in everyday/journalistic prose figures are usually written as digits, so this surfaces
mainly in spelled-out numbers and in agreement of the surrounding words. See conventions.md
for numeral systems, and for the rule that 0/1/2 are often written out rather than as digits.
