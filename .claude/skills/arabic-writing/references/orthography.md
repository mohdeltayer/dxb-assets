## 1. Hamza on its seats (الهمزة)

**Strongest-vowel rule (قاعدة أقوى الحركتين):** kasra (seat ئ) > damma (seat ؤ) >
fatha (seat أ) > sukūn (on the line, ء).

- **Initial:** always on an alif — أَكَلَ، أُمّ (fatha/damma above), إِنسان (kasra below),
  آمَنَ (madda for أ + ā).
- **Medial:** the stronger of the hamza's own vowel and the preceding letter's vowel wins —
  رئيس، طائر، سُئِل (ya seat); مسؤول، سؤال، رؤية (waw seat); سأل، مسألة، رأْس (alif seat);
  قراءة، تساءل (on the line, after a long vowel).
- **Final:** the seat follows the **preceding** vowel only — بدأ، قرأ (after fatha → alif);
  تكافؤ، لؤلؤ (after damma → waw); شاطئ، مبتدِئ، مبادئ (after kasra → ya); شيء، دفء، هدوء،
  ماء، بناء (after sukūn or a long vowel → on the line).
- Suffixes recompute the seat: زملاء + ي → زملائي؛ زملاء + نا → زملاؤنا.

## 2. Hamzat al-waṣl vs hamzat al-qaṭʿ

- **Qaṭʿ (أ / إ / ؤ / ئ):** always pronounced.  **Waṣl (a bare ا):** pronounced only at the
  start of an utterance, dropped mid-sentence.
- **The و-test:** prefix و and read aloud. If the hamza survives → qaṭʿ (وَأكل); if it vanishes
  → waṣl (واكتشف).
- **Waṣl** appears in: the ten nouns (اسم، ابن، ابنة، امرؤ، امرأة، اثنان، اثنتان، …), the past
  tense / verbal noun / imperative of 5- and 6-letter verbs (انطلق / انطلاق، استخدم / استخدام),
  the imperative of triliteral verbs (اكتب، اذهب), and the article الـ.
- **Qaṭʿ** appears in: triliteral hamza-initial verbs (أكل، أخذ), all quadriliteral verbs and
  their verbal nouns (أكرم / إكرام), all particles (إنّ، إلى، أو), and all independent pronouns
  (أنا، أنتَ).
- Errors: dropping the hamza (انسان for إنسان) or adding one (أبن for ابن، أستخدام for استخدام).

## 3. ة vs ه (taa marbuta vs haa)

**Test:** add tanwīn or a suffix / connect the word. If you hear /t/ write **ة**; if /h/ write
**ه**. مدرسةٌ (madrasatun → ة) vs إكراهٌ (→ ه). Meaning-bearing: كتابه "his book" vs كتابة
"writing"; مدرسة "a school" vs مدرسه "his teacher".

## 4. ى vs ي (alif maqsura) — الألف اللينة

- **Triliterals:** trace the root via the present tense / dual — دعا (يدعو, root و → ا) vs رمى
  (يرمي, root ي → ى).
- **4+ letters:** write ى **unless** preceded by a ya, then ا — مستشفى، مستوى، أعطى BUT دنيا،
  عليا، استحيا.
- **Particles:** all use a straight ا **except** إلى، على، حتى، بلى.
- **Homophone trap:** عليّ ("Ali," real ya) vs على (the preposition "on"); إلى ("to").
- Egyptian habit: the final ya is often written undotted (عربى، مصرى); standardized publishing
  dots it (عربي، مصري) while keeping the alif maqsura undotted (على). Apply consistently.

## 5. Initial ا vs أ

Flag a bare initial alif where qaṭʿ is required: انا → أنا، اريد → أريد، احمد → أحمد. Avoid the
reverse over-correction (أستخدام for استخدام, which is waṣl).

## 6. Tanwīn (التنوين)

- Written as a **doubled vowel mark**, never as a spelled nūn (✗ كتابن for كتابٌ).
- Tanwīn fatḥ adds a **supporting alif** — كتابًا، قلمًا — **except** after taa marbuta (شجرةً),
  after a hamza-on-the-line preceded by alif (ماءً، بناءً), or after alif maqsura (فتىً).
- The marks sit on the letter **before** the alif: رجلًا. Both house styles شكرًا and شكراً are
  attested (mark before vs on the alif); pick one and stay consistent.

## 7. Punctuation (علامات الترقيم) and spacing

- Use the **mirrored Arabic glyphs**, not the Latin ones: comma **،** (U+060C), semicolon **؛**
  (U+061B), question mark **؟** (U+061F). The period **.** is shared.
- Pause hierarchy: ، < ؛ < .  The semicolon links a clause to its cause/result: نجح الطلابُ؛
  لأنهم اجتهدوا.
- **Spacing:** no space *before* a mark, one space *after*. No space after a one-letter prefix
  (و، ف، ل، ك، ب، س): والكلمة، للمدرسة، بالقلم. No double spaces.
- **Serial lists repeat و** rather than using commas: ✓ الإسكندرية وبورسعيد والسويس.
- Colon before a quote or list; Arabic quotation marks «…» (or "…").

## 8. Diacritics (التشكيل) and kashida

- **Default to undiacritized** MSA — news, books, and UI all omit ḥarakāt. Add marks only to
  resolve genuine ambiguity (مدرسة vs مدرِّسة؛ عَلِمَ vs عُلِّمَ).
- **Always keep a meaning-distinctive shadda** (درس vs درّس). The accusative tanwīn alif
  survives even in light text (شكرًا، أهلًا).
- Full tashkīl is reserved for the Qur'an, poetry, learner/children's material, and dictionaries.
- **Never use the kashida / tatweel (ـــ)** to stretch or pad words — it breaks search and screen
  readers.

## 9. Frequent typos and homophone confusions

- Silent dagger-alif words: هذا، هذه، هؤلاء، ذلك، لكن — ✗ هاذا، ذالك، لاكن.
- **إن شاء الله** (three words; ✗ إنشاء الله = "creation of God").
- ض vs ظ flips meaning: ضلّ "went astray" vs ظلّ "remained"؛ حضر vs حظر "to ban".
- Pronoun spellings: أنتِ (not أنتي)، أنتَ (not أنتا / أنته).
- Standard forms: مسؤول (waw seat) is academy-preferred over مسئول؛ both مائة and مئة are accepted.
- Usage: نحو / زهاء for approximating a quantity (not حوالي, which means "surrounding"); شكّ
  **في** (not شكّ بـ).

## 10. Proofreading pass order

When editing existing Arabic, check in this order and report fixes grouped by category:
**hamza → ة/ه and ى/ي → tanwīn → spacing → punctuation → agreement → consistency.** Then run
the translationese smell test (translationese.md) for naturalness.
